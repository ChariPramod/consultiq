# ConsultIQ competitive research

Research date: 2026-09-09. Scope: dental enquiry review and coaching, adjacent revenue intelligence, contact-center quality assurance, and conversation practice. This is an input to the implementation and market research report, not a claim that the proposed features are already implemented.

## Recommendation

Start with a dental training lead who needs to review coordinator role-plays consistently, explain feedback, and assign focused practice. After validating that workflow, expand into approved recorded enquiries and verified follow-up outcomes. Do not compete initially as a complete phone system, autonomous receptionist, or broad revenue intelligence suite.

This recommendation is an inference from the products below and the current [ConsultIQ product plan](../PRODUCT_PLAN.md). It still needs buyer interviews and paid-pilot evidence. The market already contains dental-specific call intelligence, evidence-linked quality scoring, source-backed live assistance, and role-play generation from calls. A generic transcript summary, RAG chat window, or attractive dashboard is unlikely to be a sufficient reason to switch.

## Evidence and pricing rules

The sources are vendor product pages, vendor help documentation, and vendor pricing pages. They establish what vendors advertise or document. They do not independently verify accuracy, latency, revenue lift, compliance, or customer satisfaction. No vendor's claimed improvement is adopted as a ConsultIQ forecast. This research did not run competitive trials, inspect private contracts, or interview customers.

Prices are US-dollar public page observations on the research date unless stated otherwise. They are not quotes. Billing term, location count, included minutes, hardware, setup, integrations, compliance arrangements, taxes, and support can change the total. Where no public self-service price was found on the cited product pages, obtain a written proposal rather than relying on third-party estimates. A free product demonstration is not permission to export its customer data or reuse its training material.

## Selling points worth translating into implementation

### Enquiry recovery, with an auditable outcome

Patient Prism and Call Box frame the problem around booking outcomes and phone-process gaps. Weave makes follow-up tasks explicit. The lesson is to build an enquiry lifecycle rather than leave a manager with a score. [Patient Prism](https://www.patientprism.com/pricing/), [Call Box](https://callbox.com/dental), [Weave](https://www.getweave.com/call-intelligence/)

Proposed ConsultIQ workflow: classify an enquiry, let a reviewer confirm the barrier, assign an approved follow-up, record its completion, and reconcile the result with the practice's source of truth. Preserve distinctions between an appointment mentioned in a transcript, an appointment actually booked in the patient system, an attended visit, and collected revenue. A model's estimate must not silently become a financial fact.

Start read-only. Export an approved action or let a staff member perform it. Add writeback only with explicit access scopes, duplicate protection, an audit record, and a recovery path when the downstream system rejects a change. Automatic booking would require additional product and operational decisions.

### Review calibration and staff trust

Observe.AI documents calibration and dispute workflows; Gong documents structured scorecards and result visibility controls. These are useful reference points for professional review tooling. They are vendor-documented capabilities, not independent proof that their scores are unbiased. [Observe.AI Auto QA](https://www.observe.ai/post-interaction/auto-qa), [Gong scorecards](https://help.gong.io/docs/create-and-manage-scorecards)

ConsultIQ already preserves assessment revisions and validates quote existence. The next useful increment is blind reviewer comparison, disagreement reasons, adjudication, an acknowledgement/dispute workflow, and evaluation broken down by rubric dimension. Keep unsupported assessments visibly unsupported. A valid quotation can still be irrelevant to the rubric; semantic support needs a separate judged evaluation.

### Coaching that leads to practice

Gong and Second Nature explicitly connect evaluation with training and simulated conversations. ConsultIQ should learn from the closed workflow, while avoiding a novelty claim for AI role-play itself. [Gong Enable](https://www.gong.io/platform/sales-enablement-software), [Second Nature product](https://secondnature.ai/product/)

Proposed workflow: a reviewer chooses one approved coaching objective; the system creates a scenario from approved training content; the coordinator practices; a held-out scenario checks whether the skill transfers; the manager reviews the outcome. Keep generated scenarios separate from the evaluation set. Do not use the same model's self-rating as the only evidence of learning.

### Assistance with useful timing

Cresta's product positions source-backed guidance around the ongoing conversation and surrounding workflow. Its advertised performance language is not an end-to-end latency benchmark for ConsultIQ. [Cresta Agent Assist](https://cresta.com/agent-assist)

For ConsultIQ, first replay approved recordings through a simulated live stream and measure when an intervention would have been relevant. Then trial suggestions in shadow mode before showing them to staff. Evaluate late guidance, unnecessary prompts, missed opportunities, and incorrect guidance. A fast but distracting assistant can be a worse product than a reliable post-call reviewer.

### Integration is part of the value

Mango and Weave document writing summaries back to patient systems; CallRail documents REST API access within its tracking product. Integration claims do not mean each vendor exposes every feature through an unrestricted public API. [Mango AI](https://mangovoice.com/mango-ai), [Weave Call Intelligence](https://www.getweave.com/call-intelligence/), [CallRail plan documentation](https://support.callrail.com/hc/en-us/articles/43563657652237-CallRail-s-Plan-Types)

Select the first connector from the actual pilot practice's installed system. Obtain export/API access and document exactly which records it provides before building against it. Prefer a narrow adapter for recording metadata, transcripts and outcome reconciliation. Do not commit to supporting every dental phone and patient-management vendor at launch.

## Differentiation hypotheses to test

These are proposed advantages to earn. This research does not establish that competitors lack them.

| Hypothesis | Concrete product behavior | Evidence needed before a market claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dental coaching is easier to trust | Owner-approved rubric, evidence context, explicit abstention, staff dispute and supervisor adjudication | Blind expert agreement by dimension, unsupported-score rate, overturn reasons, and observed reviewer time |
| Training can start with little integration work | Import approved role-plays and train against an approved playbook before connecting a patient system | Observed setup time, successful independent onboarding, actual buyer willingness to pay |
| Coaching changes the targeted behavior | A reviewed call produces focused practice, then a different scenario measures transfer | Independent held-out ratings before and after; sustained use; comparison against the existing training process |
| Enquiry problems are separated from staff problems | Distinguish scheduling capacity, accepted insurance, call routing and follow-up gaps from communication behavior | Reviewer-confirmed classifications and evidence that the resulting actions help managers resolve problems |
| Knowledge can be inspected and corrected | Each suggestion names the approved policy version, effective date, supporting passage and unresolved conflict | Retrieval relevance, source freshness, citation entailment, abstention on missing or contradictory policies |
| Live support helps without interrupting work | Few contextually relevant suggestions, easy dismissal, human control, graceful fallback | Relevance and nuisance rates, time-to-useful-guidance distributions, workflow outcomes in a controlled pilot |

The strongest potential defense is accumulated permissioned domain evaluation data, useful customer integrations, reliable operational execution, and a workflow buyers repeatedly use. This is a strategy inference, not a measured moat. Data alone is not an advantage if it lacks rights, labels, coverage, or quality.

## What not to use as differentiation

- **Generic summarization or RAG.** Existing products already offer summaries and source-backed assistance. The question is whether retrieval finds the right current practice policy and whether the answer follows from it. [Mango AI](https://mangovoice.com/mango-ai), [Cresta Agent Assist](https://cresta.com/agent-assist)
- **An AI score with evidence.** Evidence-linked QA is already documented elsewhere. ConsultIQ can compete on validated usefulness, transparency, workflow and a specific buyer's needs. [Observe.AI Interaction Intelligence](https://www.observe.ai/platform/interaction-intelligence)
- **An AI receptionist merely because it sounds impressive.** Adjacent products already sell unanswered-call handling. It changes the task from reviewing staff to speaking and acting on behalf of a practice. [CallRail Voice Assist](https://www.voiceassist.callrail.com/)
- **Unverified revenue predictions, sentiment or leaderboards.** These can produce attractive screens without explaining why an outcome occurred. Evaluate the underlying construct and source data before presenting a prediction or ranking as reliable.
- **Enterprise breadth before a usable core.** Phones, scheduling, payments, quality management and training can each become a substantial product. Choose the workflow that the pilot customer will actually use and pay for.

## Buyer and competitor validation next

1. Interview a practice owner, an office manager and a training lead about their current review workflow, installed systems, recent missed enquiries, accountability and budget ownership. Ask them to demonstrate the work rather than only request features.
2. Ask which capabilities their existing vendor subscription already includes. A bundled feature changes the willingness to purchase a separate product.
3. With the owner's authorization, request written proposals for the shortlisted competitors using the same location count, staff count, monthly call volume, retention requirement, integrations, onboarding and support terms. No sales outreach was performed in this research.
4. Use the same approved role-plays and evaluation rubric in competitive trials where terms permit. Compare evidence accuracy, unsupported answers, correction effort, task completion and setup burden. Do not compare only generated prose quality.
5. Agree on the first paid-pilot acceptance criteria before collecting results. Report baseline and post-pilot results with denominators, exclusions and uncertainty. A before/after change by itself does not prove the software caused it.
6. Continue only when the buyer identifies a repeatable problem and a reason to use ConsultIQ alongside or instead of existing tools. Revisit positioning if buyers mainly want a capability already bundled into their phone platform.

## Research limits and source follow-up

The competitor pages can change after this date. Public prices need reconfirmation before procurement. The CallRail pricing page contained inconsistent Voice Assist included-call text, and a conditional Patient Prism partner offer differs from its general sales-led pricing presentation. Those inconsistencies have been retained as uncertainty rather than silently resolved.

This review has no independent product-performance benchmark or representative customer interviews. It establishes a competitive feature baseline and a testable direction. Private API access, export rights, exact data retention, healthcare processing arrangements and contract terms must be checked directly for the chosen deployment and account. The source pages' certification or compliance language has not been audited here and does not establish ConsultIQ's readiness.
