# ConsultIQ: Consultation Call Intelligence and Case Acceptance Scorer

**One line:** Upload a treatment consultation recording; get a diarized transcript, an 8-dimension rubric score with quoted evidence and coaching notes, a calibrated case acceptance probability with the top contributing factors, and cross-call pattern mining. Every score is explainable and the tool documents what it cannot see.

**Target timeline:** 2 to 3 weeks after ArcAgent ships (roughly 50 to 70 hours). No voice infrastructure, so it moves faster.
**Build order:** Second of three.

---

## 1. Problem and buyer

10x Dental runs The Closing Experts Academy because case acceptance is where the money leaks. A practice spends on ads, generates leads, books consultations, and closes a fraction of them. The person in the room, the treatment coordinator, decides the close rate. Coaching today is subjective (whoever the manager listened to), infrequent (a few calls a month), and inconsistent (no shared rubric).

The buyer is a sales training business or a DSO with five or more coordinators. What they want is a consistent, scalable, evidence-backed way to evaluate every consultation and tell each coordinator exactly what to work on.

**Who else has this problem:** any sales training company, legal intake, real estate, insurance, financial advisory, high-ticket e-commerce with live sales. This is a horizontal product with a dental demo.

## 2. Scope

### In scope (v1)

- Web upload of MP3/MP4/WAV recordings and paste-in of existing transcripts
- Batch transcription with speaker diarization (two speakers: coordinator and patient), with automatic role assignment
- Transcript preprocessing: timestamps, turn segmentation, talk-time ratio, question counts, silence detection
- Rubric scoring engine: 8 dimensions, 1 to 5 each, with verbatim quoted evidence that is verified to exist in the transcript, and a one-line coaching note per dimension
- Case acceptance prediction: a calibrated classifier trained on a synthetic labeled dataset, outputting probability, uncertainty band, and top contributing factors
- Pattern mining across a batch: phrases and behaviors that correlate with acceptance, unrecovered objections, time-to-price comparison
- Streamlit dashboard: per-call report, coordinator comparison, batch trends, filters
- Evaluation: inter-rater reliability (LLM vs human) with Cohen's kappa per dimension, judge consistency across repeated runs, adversarial transcript tests
- A written "what the rubric cannot see" limitations document

### Out of scope (v1)

- Real patient call data (you will not have it and should not seek it for a portfolio project)
- Real-time in-call coaching
- Integration with call recording platforms (RingCentral, Weave, etc.)
- Multi-language transcripts
- Video analysis (facial expression, body language)
- Fine-grained PHI redaction in transcripts (note it as a production requirement)

### Stretch

- Fine-tuned DistilBERT on transcript text as a second model, compared to the feature-based baseline
- Coordinator-specific trend lines over time
- A "coach mode" that generates a role-play script targeting a coordinator's weakest dimension
- Export a one-page PDF coaching report per call

## 3. Decisions and considerations before you write code

### 3.1 You have no real data, and how you handle that is the whole story

Every consultation recording is PHI. You will not get real ones. Your options:

1. **Synthetic transcripts generated by an LLM** from structured scenario specs (coordinator skill profile, patient persona, outcome). This is your primary dataset.
2. **Recorded role-plays** with a friend or with yourself playing both parts, for a small set of real audio files to test the transcription and diarization pipeline. Even 5 to 10 of these are worth doing because synthetic transcripts skip the hardest part (audio).
3. **Public dental sales training content** (YouTube role-plays, published case acceptance methodology) to ground the rubric. Use it to design dimensions, not as training data.

The critical risk with synthetic data is **circularity**: if the same model generates transcripts with a known outcome label and then judges them, the classifier can learn generator artifacts rather than sales behavior. Mitigations, all of which you should implement and describe:

- Generate with one model family and judge with a different one
- Generate from structured specs where the coordinator's behaviors are controlled independently of the outcome label, with deliberate noise (some skilled coordinators lose cases, some weak ones close)
- Inject realistic messiness: filler words, crosstalk, patients who change the subject, coordinators who ramble
- Hold out an entire generation "style" (different prompt template, different model) as a test set to check the classifier generalizes across generators
- Hand-label a subset yourself, blind to the generator's label, and report agreement

State plainly in the README that the acceptance model is trained on synthetic data and its absolute accuracy numbers do not transfer to real calls. What transfers is the pipeline, the rubric, and the evaluation methodology. This honesty is a feature; it is what a hiring manager wants to hear.

### 3.2 Rubric design

Draw the 8 dimensions from published case acceptance methodology, and cite what you drew from in `docs/rubric_design.md`. Proposed set:

1. **Needs discovery:** did the coordinator ask open questions about the patient's goals and history before presenting treatment?
2. **Emotional acknowledgment:** did they name and respond to fear, embarrassment, or frustration rather than moving past it?
3. **Presentation clarity:** plain language vs jargon; did they check understanding?
4. **Price anchoring:** when was cost introduced relative to value, and was it framed (total cost of inaction, financing) or dropped bare?
5. **Objection response:** did they address "I need to think about it," "it's too expensive," "I'm scared" with a real response rather than a pause or a concession?
6. **Call to action:** was the next step specific, dated, and confirmed by the patient?
7. **Social proof and reassurance:** patient stories, success rates, financing options, guarantees
8. **Follow-up commitment:** was a concrete follow-up scheduled and owned by someone?

For each dimension the scoring engine outputs: `score` (1 to 5), `evidence` (a list of verbatim spans with turn indices), `rationale` (2 sentences), `coaching_note` (one actionable sentence). Write a scoring guide per dimension with anchor descriptions for 1, 3, and 5, so the LLM judge and the human rater use the same standard. This guide is what makes the kappa measurement meaningful.

### 3.3 Evidence must be verifiable

LLM judges hallucinate quotes. Every evidence span returned must be validated by exact or near-exact substring match against the transcript. If a span does not match, discard it, log it, and if a dimension ends up with no valid evidence, flag the score as unsupported. Report the hallucinated-quote rate in your eval. This is a small piece of engineering that says a lot.

### 3.4 Judge consistency

Score the same transcript 5 times with the same prompt. Report per-dimension standard deviation. If a dimension has high variance, its scoring guide is ambiguous; fix the guide, not the model. Use temperature 0 and structured outputs, but still measure, because structured outputs do not eliminate variance.

### 3.5 Diarization and role assignment

- Options: Deepgram batch with `diarize=true` (fastest to integrate, paid), or WhisperX with pyannote (local, free, heavier setup, needs a Hugging Face token for pyannote).
- Use Deepgram for the main path and keep a WhisperX adapter behind the same interface. Being able to say "swappable transcription backend" is worth the small effort.
- Role assignment: the coordinator usually speaks first, asks more questions, and says the practice name. A small LLM call over the first 10 turns is reliable enough. Log the confidence and let the user flip roles in the UI.

### 3.6 The prediction model: start simple, add complexity only with evidence

**Baseline:** logistic regression over engineered features. Features:
- 8 rubric scores
- Talk-time ratio (coordinator vs patient)
- Number of open questions asked before first treatment mention
- Time (turn index and seconds) to first price mention
- Count of objections detected and count recovered
- Whether a specific next step with a date was confirmed
- Call duration
- Patient sentiment trajectory (first third vs last third)

**Second model:** DistilBERT fine-tuned on the transcript text (or on coordinator turns only). Compare to the baseline. If it does not beat the baseline on the held-out generator style, say so and keep the baseline as the shipped model. That result is interesting either way.

**Calibration and uncertainty:** apply Platt scaling or isotonic regression on a calibration split; report a reliability diagram. For the uncertainty band, bootstrap the training set 200 times and report the 10th to 90th percentile of predicted probability. For contributing factors, use coefficients for logistic regression or SHAP for anything more complex. Show the top 2 in the UI.

### 3.7 Pattern mining

Over a batch of scored calls:
- Phrase-level: TF-IDF n-grams from coordinator turns, compared between accepted and not-accepted groups, with a significance test and a minimum support threshold so you do not report noise
- Objection-level: cluster detected objections by embedding, then compute recovery rate per cluster; the cluster with the lowest recovery rate is the coaching priority
- Timing: distribution of time-to-price for accepted vs not-accepted; the "how early is too early" chart is a strong demo visual
- Dimension-level: which rubric dimension has the largest gap between the top and bottom quartile of coordinators

### 3.8 Data handling

Even with synthetic data, design as if it were real: transcripts stored in Postgres with an owner ID, audio files deleted after transcription by default (config flag to keep), PII fields identified in the schema, retention config. Write `docs/data_handling.md`.

```
   |
   v

   |
   v

   |--> Per-call report
   |--> Coordinator comparison
   |--> Batch pattern mining

Offline
   |--> Synthetic data generator (spec -> transcript -> label)
   |--> Training and calibration scripts

```

## 6. Implementation plan

### Week 1: data and transcription

**Days 1 to 2: Rubric and scenario design**
- Write `docs/rubric_design.md`: 8 dimensions, anchor descriptions for scores 1, 3, 5, sources you drew from
- Write the synthetic scenario spec schema (YAML): coordinator skill profile (a value per dimension), patient persona (goals, fear level, budget, insurance), objections planned, messiness level, outcome label, and the rule that decides the label from skill and persona plus noise
- Write 10 scenario specs by hand to get the shape right

**Days 3 to 4: Synthetic transcript generator**
- Generator script: spec plus template plus model → diarized transcript with timestamps
- Two generation styles (different template, different model) so you can hold one out
- Generate 300 transcripts: 240 in style A, 60 in style B; balance outcomes roughly 40/60
- Spot-read 20. If they all sound the same, add more messiness and persona variety before proceeding.

**Day 5: Audio pipeline**
- Record 5 to 10 role-play calls (phone-quality audio, some background noise)
- Deepgram batch diarization adapter, WhisperX adapter behind the same interface
- Role assignment step with confidence
- Test both adapters on the role-plays; note diarization errors in `failure_modes.md`

**Weekend checkpoint:** 300 synthetic transcripts in Postgres, audio-to-transcript path working on real recordings.

### Week 2: scoring and modeling

**Days 6 to 7: Rubric scoring engine**
- Structured-output prompt using the scoring guide; decide between one call per dimension (more consistent, more expensive) and one call for all eight (cheaper); measure both on 20 transcripts and pick
- Evidence validator with fuzzy substring matching (handle whitespace and punctuation drift); log hallucinated spans
- Score all 300 transcripts; store with prompt version

**Day 8: Judge consistency and human ratings**
- Score 20 transcripts 5 times each; compute per-dimension standard deviation
- Hand-rate 30 transcripts yourself using the scoring guide, blind to LLM scores; store in `human_ratings`
- Compute Cohen's kappa (weighted, since scores are ordinal) per dimension; write the results table

**Day 9: Feature extraction and baseline model**
- Feature extraction module with unit tests on a hand-built transcript
- Logistic regression pipeline, stratified train/calibration/test split within style A, plus style B as the out-of-generator test
- Calibration, reliability diagram, bootstrap band, coefficient-based factor attribution

**Day 10: Second model and comparison**
- DistilBERT fine-tune on coordinator turns (small run, a few epochs, on CPU or a free GPU tier)
- Compare AUC and calibration on both test sets; write `docs/model_comparison.md` with the honest result

**Weekend checkpoint:** every synthetic call has rubric scores with validated evidence and a calibrated acceptance probability; kappa and judge variance tables exist.

### Week 3: patterns, dashboard, evaluation, shipping

**Day 11: Pattern mining**
- N-gram contrast with significance and support thresholds
- Objection clustering and recovery rate per cluster
- Time-to-price distributions by outcome
- Dimension gap by coordinator quartile

**Days 12 to 13: Streamlit dashboard**
- Upload page (audio or pasted transcript) that runs the full pipeline and shows a report
- Per-call report: transcript with highlighted evidence spans, 8 dimension cards with score, evidence, coaching note; acceptance probability with band and top factors
- Batch page: filters by coordinator, date, lead source; pattern mining charts
- Coordinator page: per-dimension averages, trend, weakest dimension

**Day 14: Adversarial tests and limitations doc**
- Adversarial transcripts: heavy crosstalk, long silences, coordinator who is polite but never asks for the close, patient who says yes early then reverses, transcript with diarization errors (roles swapped for a stretch)
- Record how each dimension behaves; some will fail, document it
- Write `docs/what_the_rubric_cannot_see.md`: body language, in-room paperwork, tone beyond words, pre-existing relationship, what happened after the call, financing conversations that happen with a different staff member. Then a section on what the synthetic data cannot tell you.

**Days 15 to 16: Ship**
- README: problem, pipeline diagram, rubric summary, kappa table, judge variance table, model comparison table (with the caveat about synthetic data in bold), pattern mining screenshots, limitations, how to run
- Loom (3 minutes): upload a role-play recording, watch the report populate, click into evidence spans, show the coordinator comparison, end on the limitations doc
- Tag a release

## 7. Evaluation in detail

| Evaluation | What it answers | How to report |
|---|---|---|
| Weighted Cohen's kappa per dimension, LLM vs you (30 calls) | Does the judge agree with a careful human using the same guide? | Table with kappa and interpretation band; note that n=30 and one rater |
| Judge consistency (20 calls, 5 repeats) | Is the score stable enough to coach on? | Per-dimension SD; list dimensions above 0.5 SD as needing guide revision |
| Hallucinated evidence rate | Can the quotes be trusted? | Percent of spans that failed validation, before and after prompt fixes |
| Acceptance model AUC and Brier on in-style test | Does it learn the signal in the synthetic data? | Number plus reliability diagram |
| Acceptance model AUC on out-of-style test | Does it generalize past one generator? | Number; if it drops a lot, say the model is generator-sensitive |
| Baseline vs DistilBERT | Is the complexity earned? | Side by side; ship whichever wins on out-of-style |
| Adversarial set (8 to 10 transcripts) | Where does it break? | Per-case notes in `failure_modes.md` |
| Role assignment accuracy on role-plays | Does the pipeline get the basics right on real audio? | Count correct out of total |

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Synthetic transcripts are homogeneous and the classifier is trivially accurate | Two generation styles, held-out style, messiness injection, hand review; report out-of-style numbers front and center |
| LLM judge agrees with you because you both read the same guide, not because it is right | Say exactly that in the README; kappa measures consistency of application, not ground truth |
| Diarization fails on real audio | Keep the role-play set small, document errors, offer manual role flip in UI |
| Pyannote setup eats a day | Deepgram first, WhisperX as the optional adapter |
| DistilBERT fine-tune is slow on CPU | Small model, coordinator turns only, 2 to 3 epochs, or skip if time is short and note it |
| Dashboard scope creep | Three pages, no more |
